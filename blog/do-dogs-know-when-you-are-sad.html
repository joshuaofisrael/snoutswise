Do dogs know when you are sad? They respond to crying

Published 30 October 2026 · Last reviewed 30 October 2026 · By SnoutsWise Editorial Team

Many owners say their dog comes over when they are upset. Research backs part of that up. A 2012 study found dogs approached people who were pretending to cry more than people who were talking or humming, and a 2016 study found dogs can match a happy or angry human voice to the matching face. Whether dogs feel sympathy the way people do is still an open question.

Dogs go to people who are crying

In the 2012 study in Animal Cognition, researchers at Goldsmiths, University of London adapted a test first used with human babies. Each dog's owner and a stranger took turns talking, humming and pretending to cry. Dogs looked at or went to the person more often when that person was crying than when they were talking or humming.

They comforted strangers too

When the stranger pretended to cry, dogs did not run to their owner for comfort. They went to the stranger instead and sniffed, nuzzled and licked them. Observers who did not know what the study was testing more often described the dogs' approaches during crying as submissive rather than playful, alert or calm.

Is it empathy?

The authors say the dogs behaved in a way that fits empathic concern, but that the simplest explanation is emotional contagion (picking up on someone else's emotional state) plus learning: many dogs have been rewarded in the past for going to upset people. So the study shows dogs respond to crying, not that they understand sadness as we do.

Dogs can match voices to faces

A 2016 study in Biology Letters, by a team from the University of Lincoln and the University of São Paulo, showed dogs two photos of the same person or dog side by side, one happy or playful and one angry or aggressive, while playing a single sound. Dogs looked longer at the face whose expression matched the emotion in the voice, for both human and dog faces. The researchers say this means dogs combine what they see and hear to tell positive from negative emotions, an ability previously known only in humans.

What this means for owners

  • Your dog notices your mood. Changes in your voice, face and behavior are signals your dog can pick up on. See our guide to dog body language for the other side of the conversation.
  • A stressed household can stress a dog. If your dog seems anxious when people are upset, give them a quiet space and keep routines steady.
  • Not a replacement for support. Dogs can be good company, but if you are struggling, talk to someone you trust or a health professional.

Frequently asked questions

Do dogs know when you are sad?

Dogs respond to signs of sadness. In a 2012 study, dogs approached people more when they pretended to cry than when they talked or hummed, and they often nuzzled or licked them. Whether dogs understand sadness the way people do is not known.

Do dogs comfort people who are crying?

Often, yes. In the 2012 study, dogs went to a crying stranger rather than to their own owner, and sniffed, nuzzled and licked them. The authors think learning and emotional contagion explain this best.

Can dogs tell if you are happy or angry?

A 2016 study found dogs looked longer at a happy or angry human face when it matched the emotion of a voice they heard, which suggests they can tell positive from negative emotions in people.
