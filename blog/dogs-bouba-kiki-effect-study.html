News: dogs match "bouba" to round shapes and "kiki" to spiky ones

Published 16 October 2026 · Last reviewed 16 October 2026 · By the SnoutsWise team

People around the world tend to call a rounded shape "bouba" and a spiky shape "kiki". A study published in Proceedings of the Royal Society B on 14 October 2026 found that adult pet dogs show a similar link: they looked more at a rounded shape while "bouba" was played and looked at and explored a spiky shape while "kiki" was played. The authors say this suggests the sound and shape link is not unique to human language.

What the researchers did

Eleonora Biffi and colleagues, led from the University of Parma in Italy according to New Scientist, showed pet dogs a rounded shape on one side of a room and a spiky shape on the other while the nonsense words were played. In the first experiment, 51 owners sat holding their adult dogs. New Scientist reports that 56% of dogs lingered on the round shape when they heard "bouba" and 63% focused on the spiky shape when they heard "kiki".

To rule out owners giving hidden cues, the team repeated the test with owners wearing headphones playing music so they could not hear the words. The result held, with a slightly stronger bias toward the round shape for "bouba".

Why it matters

The bouba-kiki effect has long been described in people as a case of sound symbolism linked to language. The paper notes that 3-day-old chicks also show it, and the dog result adds to evidence that it may be a broader matching between hearing and vision rather than something language creates. In New Scientist, Biffi said it may reflect general principles of how the brain combines information from different senses, while Attila Andics at Eotvos Lorand University, who was not an author, said a next question is whether dogs learn these links or are born with the expectation.

The authors say more work is needed on what the link is for and how it develops. Biffi told New Scientist the team is now testing puppies and wild canids such as foxes, and would like to test cats.

What it means for owners

  • Nothing to change. This is basic research on perception, not a training method.
  • Dogs process human sounds in rich ways. It fits other research showing dogs pay close attention to how we speak (see why dogs tilt their heads).
  • Clear, consistent cue words still help. Short, distinct words for each cue are easier for dogs to learn (see reward based training).

Frequently asked questions

What is the bouba-kiki effect?

The tendency to match the made-up word bouba with a rounded shape and kiki with a spiky shape. It has been reported in people across many cultures and in young chicks.

Do dogs show the bouba-kiki effect?

A 2026 Proceedings B study found adult pet dogs looked more at a rounded shape when bouba was played and at a spiky shape when kiki was played, even when owners could not hear the words.

Does this mean dogs understand language?

No. The authors and outside experts say it points to general links between hearing and vision rather than language. It is not yet known whether dogs learn the link or are born with it.
