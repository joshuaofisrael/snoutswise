Why do dogs make puppy dog eyes? A muscle wolves mostly lack

Published 28 October 2026 · Last reviewed 28 October 2026 · By SnoutsWise Editorial Team

That pleading look, with the inner eyebrows raised and the eyes looking bigger, has a real anatomical basis. A 2019 study in PNAS found that dogs have a small muscle for raising the inner eyebrow that wolves do not have in the same way, and a 2017 study found that dogs make more facial movements when a person is paying attention to them.

A muscle for raising the eyebrows

In the 2019 study, researchers from the UK and US dissected the heads of dogs and wolves. A muscle called the levator anguli oculi medialis, which raises the inner eyebrow intensely, was present in all the dogs studied but not in the wolves in the same way.

Dogs use the movement more than wolves

The team also filmed dogs and wolves meeting a person. Dogs raised their inner eyebrows more often and more intensely than wolves did, and the most intense movements were seen only in dogs.

Why it might matter to people

The movement makes the eyes look bigger and more baby-like, and it resembles the face humans make when sad. The authors suggest it may trigger a nurturing response in people. Their hypothesis is that, during domestication, dogs with expressive eyebrows had an advantage and that puppy dog eyes are the result of selection based on human preferences. This is a hypothesis, not a proven cause.

Dogs make more faces when we are watching

A 2017 study in Scientific Reports, from the University of Portsmouth, placed dogs in front of a person who was either facing them or turned away, with or without food. Dogs made significantly more facial movements, including the eyebrow raise, when the person was paying attention. Food on its own made no difference. The researchers say this suggests dog facial expressions may be active attempts to communicate, not just automatic displays of emotion.

What this means for owners

  • The look is partly aimed at you. It is one way dogs communicate with people, so read it together with the rest of your dog's body language.
  • It is not proof of guilt or sadness. The studies measured facial movements, not feelings. See also what research says about dog emotions.
  • Puppy eyes at mealtimes are not hunger proof. Stick to a measured portion with our dog feeding calculator and keep treats to about 10 percent of daily calories.

Frequently asked questions

Why do dogs make puppy dog eyes?

A 2019 PNAS study found dogs have a muscle that raises the inner eyebrow intensely, which wolves lack in the same form. The authors suggest people may have favored dogs with this expression during domestication.

Do wolves make puppy dog eyes?

Rarely. In the 2019 study, wolves raised their inner eyebrows less often and less intensely than dogs, and the most intense movements were seen only in dogs.

Do dogs make faces on purpose?

A 2017 study found dogs made more facial movements when a person was watching them than when the person was turned away, which suggests the expressions may be partly meant to communicate.
