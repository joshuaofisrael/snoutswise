Can dogs sense heat with their nose? A 2020 study says yes

Published 25 October 2026 · Last reviewed 25 October 2026 · By the SnoutsWise team

The bare, often moist skin at the tip of a dog's nose, called the rhinarium, is cold and packed with nerves. A 2020 study in Scientific Reports found that dogs can tell a warm object from a neutral one by its radiating heat alone, and that warm stimuli light up a part of the dog brain that handles touch and body sensation. The authors call it a sensory ability not described before in a carnivore.

What the rhinarium is

The rhinarium is the naked, often moist skin on the tip of the nose. In dogs it is prominent and richly supplied with nerves, which suggested to researchers that it does more than help with smell. The study also notes that the nose tips of carnivores are considerably colder than those of plant eaters such as horses and cattle, and the team guessed that this coldness could make the dog nose especially sensitive to radiant heat.

The training test

Researchers in Hungary and Sweden trained three dogs to choose between two objects placed at a distance. One object was about room temperature. The other was warmed to about the surface temperature of a furry mammal. The dogs could not tell them apart by sight or smell, only by the heat the warm one gave off, and they learned to pick it out.

The brain scans

The team then scanned 13 awake, trained dogs with functional MRI while showing them heat stimuli at similar temperatures. The warm stimulus caused a stronger response in the left somatosensory association cortex, a brain area that processes body sensations, than the neutral one did.

What this does and does not show

The study shows that dogs can detect weak radiant heat and that their brains respond to it. It does not prove how dogs use this sense in daily life. The authors suggest it may have helped dogs' wild ancestors find warm prey, but that idea still needs testing, and the behaviour experiment used only three dogs.

What this means for owners

  • The nose is a whole sensing organ. Beyond smell, the nose tip also seems to pick up warmth, one more reason dogs explore the world nose first (see dog behavior).
  • Health questions go to your vet. This research is about sensing, not health, so ask your vet about any change in your dog's nose such as cracking, bleeding or discharge (see dog health).

Frequently asked questions

Can dogs sense heat with their nose?

Yes. A 2020 Scientific Reports study trained dogs to pick a warm object from a neutral one by its radiant heat alone.

Why is a dog's nose cold?

The study notes that the nose tips of carnivores like dogs are much colder than those of plant eaters, and suggests the coldness may help the nose detect radiant heat.

What part of the dog brain responds to heat?

In brain scans of 13 awake dogs, warm stimuli caused a stronger response in the left somatosensory association cortex.
