Do dogs like baby talk? Puppies do, and adult dogs like it with dog words

Published 27 October 2026 · Last reviewed 27 October 2026 · By SnoutsWise Editorial Team

Most people talk to dogs in a higher, slower, sing-song voice, much like the way adults speak to babies. Studies suggest it is not wasted effort. A 2017 study found that puppies react strongly to this dog-directed speech, and a 2018 study found that adult dogs paid more attention to, and preferred to spend time near, a person using it, as long as the words were about dogs too.

We all do it

In a 2017 study in Proceedings of the Royal Society B, French and British researchers recorded adults speaking to photos of puppies, adult dogs and old dogs. People used dog-directed speech with dogs of every age. The voice pattern was much the same for all ages, except that pitch went highest for puppies.

Puppies respond strongly

The team then played recordings to dogs with no person in sight. Puppies were highly reactive to dog-directed speech, and pitch was a key factor in how they responded. Older dogs in this setup did not react any differently to dog-directed speech than to normal speech.

Adult dogs prefer it in real life

A 2018 study in Animal Cognition from the University of York used a more natural setup, with real speakers in the room. Adult dogs paid more attention to, and showed more friendly behavior toward, a person using dog-directed speech than a person using normal adult speech.

It is the voice plus the words

In a second experiment the researchers mixed things up, using a dog-directed voice with ordinary adult words and a normal voice with dog words like "good boy" and "walkies". Neither mix made a clear difference on its own. The authors suggest it is the combination of the sing-song voice and dog-relevant words that dogs like.

What this means for owners

  • Talking to your dog in a warm, high voice is fine. It may help get a puppy's attention and may strengthen your bond with an adult dog.
  • Use words that matter to your dog. In the 2018 study, the voice worked best together with dog-related words. This fits with reward based training and with research on dogs learning words.
  • Watch your dog's response. Every dog is different, so notice what gets a relaxed, interested reaction (see dog behavior and why dogs tilt their heads).

Frequently asked questions

Do dogs like baby talk?

Studies suggest many do. A 2017 study found puppies react strongly to high-pitched dog-directed speech, and a 2018 study found adult dogs paid more attention to and preferred a person using it with dog-related words.

Why do people talk to dogs in a high voice?

A 2017 study found people use dog-directed speech with dogs of all ages, with the highest pitch for puppies. The authors suggest it is a natural way of trying to connect with listeners who do not speak.

Do older dogs respond to dog-directed speech?

In recorded playback tests, older dogs did not react differently, but in a 2018 study with real speakers, adult dogs preferred a person using dog-directed speech with dog words.
