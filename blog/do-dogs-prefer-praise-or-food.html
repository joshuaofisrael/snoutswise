Do dogs prefer praise or food? Most work harder for treats

Published 1 November 2026 · Last reviewed 1 November 2026 · By SnoutsWise Editorial Team

It depends on the dog, and on what you measure. A 2016 brain scan study found that in most of the 15 dogs tested, the brain's reward area responded about as strongly, or more strongly, to a signal for praise as to a signal for food. But in a 2012 training study, brief petting and praise were a much weaker reward than a small piece of food for most shelter dogs, pet dogs and hand-reared wolves.

What dogs' brains show

In the 2016 study in Social Cognitive and Affective Neuroscience, researchers at Emory University trained 15 dogs to lie still, awake, in an MRI scanner. Different objects told each dog that food, praise from its owner, or nothing was coming. The researchers measured activity in the ventral caudate, a brain area linked with reward. In 13 of the 15 dogs, the caudate responded about as much or more to the praise signal as to the food signal.

Food or owner? Dogs differed

The same dogs then chose, again and again, between a bowl of food and their owner at the ends of a Y-shaped maze. Dogs whose brains had responded more to praise went to their owner more often, and dogs whose brains had responded more to food went to the bowl more often. The authors say preference was stable within each dog but varied a lot between dogs.

In training, food usually wins

A 2012 study in the Journal of the Experimental Analysis of Behavior, by researchers at the University of Florida, tested how hard dogs would work for each reward. Dogs and hand-reared wolves had to touch their nose to a target. For most of them, a few seconds of social interaction from a person produced fewer responses and slower responses than a small piece of food did. This held for shelter dogs, owned dogs and wolves.

Why the studies seem to disagree

They measured different things. The brain scan study looked at how rewarding a signal felt and which option a dog walked to. The training study looked at how much effort a dog would put in for a brief reward. A dog can enjoy praise and still find a treat a stronger reason to repeat a trick.

What this means for owners

  • Use food when teaching something new. For most dogs, small soft treats are the strongest everyday reward. See our guide to reward based training.
  • Pair praise with treats. Praise is cheap and always with you, so pair it with food and keep using it once a behavior is learned.
  • Watch your dog. Some dogs really do prefer attention or play. Notice what your own dog chooses.
  • Count the treats. Keep treats to about 10 percent of daily calories with our dog feeding calculator.

Frequently asked questions

Do dogs prefer praise or food?

It varies by dog. In a 2016 brain scan study, 13 of 15 dogs showed about as much or more reward-area activity for a praise signal as for a food signal. But in a 2012 training study, most dogs worked harder for a small piece of food than for brief praise and petting.

Is praise a good reward for dogs?

Praise helps, but on its own it was a weak reward for most dogs in a 2012 study, compared with a small treat. Pairing praise with food is a common approach in reward based training.

Do some dogs like their owners more than food?

Some do. In the 2016 study, dogs whose brains responded more to praise chose their owner over a bowl of food more often in a Y-maze test.
