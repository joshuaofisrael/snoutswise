Why do dogs tilt their heads? It may mean they are listening hard

Published 12 October 2026 · Last reviewed 12 October 2026 · By SnoutsWise Editorial Team

Dogs often tilt their heads when they hear a word that means something to them. In a 2021 study of 40 dogs in Animal Cognition, dogs that had learned the names of their toys tilted their heads on 43% of requests to fetch a toy, compared with just 2% for typical dogs given the same training. The researchers think the tilt may be linked to paying close attention to meaningful speech, and each dog tended to tilt to the same side every time. A tilt that does not go away, or comes with stumbling, circling or flicking eyes, is different: that can be a balance (vestibular) problem and needs a vet.

The first study of head tilting in dogs

Head tilting is one of the most familiar dog behaviors, yet until recently no study had described it. A team at the Department of Ethology at Eötvös Loránd University in Budapest, led by Andrea Sommese and Claudia Fugazza, filmed 40 dogs during tests in which owners asked their dog to fetch a particular toy by name, for example "bring rope!". The toys were in another room, so the dog had to rely on the word alone.

The dogs fell into two groups. Seven were "gifted word learners", rare dogs that pick up the names of toys quickly. The other 33 were typical family dogs that loved toys. All of them went through the same three month training program to learn the names of two new toys, with the same weekly coaching for their owners.

What the researchers found:

  • 43% vs 2%. Across the monthly tests, the gifted word learners tilted their heads on 43% of trials. The typical dogs tilted on only 2%.
  • Familiar is not enough. The typical dogs had heard the toy names just as often over three months, so simply knowing the sound of a word did not trigger tilting. The authors suggest the tilt is linked to hearing a word that is meaningful to the dog, and may be a sign of increased attention.
  • Each dog has a side. A dog's tilt direction stayed consistent across tests months apart, so the authors treat it as a stable individual trait, a bit like paw preference.
  • It is not about where the sound comes from. Whether the owner stood to the left, right or in front made no difference to the direction of the tilt.

The study was small and exploratory. All six gifted dogs that took part in the later tests were Border collies, but so were 18 of the typical dogs that rarely tilted, so the authors warn against linking frequent tilting to that breed.

How rare are dogs that learn words?

Very rare. In a companion study in Scientific Reports, the same team gave 34 ordinary family dogs (puppies and adults, all keen on toys) three months of intensive play based training to learn two toy names. Only one of them passed the tests. The six already "word knowledgeable" dogs, found after a two year search around the world, all passed and went on to learn between 2 and 13 new toy names a month. Most dogs respond to tone of voice, routines and gestures rather than to the names of objects, and that is completely normal (see our guide to dog behavior).

Do dogs process speech on one side of the brain?

There are clues that they might. In a 2014 study in Current Biology, Victoria Ratcliffe and David Reby at the University of Sussex played dogs recordings of a familiar command from speakers on both sides and noted which way each dog turned its head. Dogs turned one way when the meaningful sounds of the words were made more noticeable, and the other way when the tone of voice and speaker cues were boosted. Because each side of the head is wired mainly to the opposite side of the brain, the authors concluded that dogs may, like people, handle the words and the emotional tone of speech in different brain hemispheres. Head turning is not the same movement as head tilting, but the Budapest team cites this work as one reason to think the side a dog tilts to could reflect how its brain processes speech. That link has not been proven yet.

Other ideas you may have heard

You will often read that dogs tilt their heads to see past their muzzle or to work out where a sound is coming from. These are reasonable guesses, but we have not found published studies that test them, so treat them as unproven. The one thing the 2021 study did check, the owner's position, made no difference to the direction of the tilt.

When a head tilt is a reason to call the vet

A quick, curious tilt when you talk to your dog is normal. A tilt that stays there is not. A constant head tilt is a classic sign of a problem with the vestibular system, the inner ear and brain parts that control balance. A 2023 review in Frontiers in Veterinary Science describes idiopathic vestibular syndrome, a common cause in dogs over nine, as usually starting suddenly with a head tilt toward the affected side, abnormal eye movements, a wobbly, unbalanced walk and sometimes nausea or vomiting. Many dogs improve within days and recover within two to four weeks, but a slight tilt can remain. Vets only diagnose it after ruling out other causes such as middle or inner ear infection, stroke, inflammation of the brain, injury and tumors.

Contact your vet promptly if your dog has any of these:

  • A head tilt that does not go away when you stop talking.
  • Stumbling, leaning, falling or circling to one side.
  • Eyes flicking side to side or up and down on their own.
  • Head shaking, scratching at an ear, ear odor or discharge.
  • Vomiting, drooling, a drooping face or seeming dull or confused (see our signs that need a vet).

Fun ways to use your dog's head tilt

  • Notice what triggers it. If your dog tilts for "walk", "ball" or a favorite toy's name, it may be telling you which words matter to it most.
  • Teach a toy name the kind way. Short, playful sessions where you name a toy while you play with it are how word learning dogs picked up names. Do not worry if your dog never learns any; most do not (see reward based training).
  • Reward calm attention. A tilt means your dog is focused on you, which is a great moment for a treat and praise.

Frequently asked questions

Why do dogs tilt their heads when you talk to them?

Probably because they are paying close attention to words that mean something to them. In a 2021 study of 40 dogs, dogs that knew the names of their toys tilted their heads on 43% of requests to fetch a toy, compared with 2% for typical dogs.

Do smarter dogs tilt their heads more?

In the one study so far, dogs that were unusually good at learning toy names tilted much more often. That suggests a link with processing meaningful words, but it does not mean a dog that never tilts is less intelligent.

Why does my dog always tilt its head to the same side?

That is normal. The 2021 study found each dog's tilt direction stayed the same across tests months apart, so researchers treat it as a stable individual trait, like paw preference.

Can all dogs learn the names of toys?

Very few can. In a 2021 study, only 1 of 34 typical family dogs learned two toy names after three months of training, while a handful of rare gifted dogs learned many.

When is a head tilt a sign of a health problem?

When it stays even when nothing is going on, or comes with stumbling, circling, falling, flicking eyes, head shaking or vomiting. These can be signs of vestibular (balance) disease or an inner ear problem, so contact your vet.
