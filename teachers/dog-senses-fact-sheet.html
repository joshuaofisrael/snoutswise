A printable fact sheet for grades 3 to 5 on how dogs see and how they show feelings, with a five question quiz and answer key.

Dog senses and body language: fact sheet

How dogs see

  • Dogs do not see only in black and white. They can make out yellow and blue, and mixes of those colors (American Kennel Club).
  • Dog eyes have two kinds of cone cells for color. Most people have three. Scientists call this dichromatic vision (Neitz, Geist and Jacobs, 1989).
  • In one experiment, dogs relied on color, not brightness, to choose between colored targets (Kasparson and colleagues, 2013).
  • Dogs have more rod cells than cone cells. Rods work in low light and help catch movement (American Kennel Club).
  • Red and orange toys are hard for dogs to see on green grass; yellow and blue are easier (American Kennel Club).

How dogs show feelings

  • A relaxed dog has a loose body, an open relaxed mouth and ears in a natural position (RSPCA).
  • A play bow, chest down and bottom up, invites play (ASPCApro).
  • A worried dog may hold its body and head low, tuck its tail, put its ears back, yawn or lick its lips (RSPCA, ASPCApro).
  • A stiff body, raised hair and a stiff, high tail are warnings to give a dog space (RSPCA).

Quick quiz

  1. How many kinds of cone cells do dogs have? ______
  2. Name two colors dogs can see well. ______ and ______
  3. What kind of eye cell helps dogs notice movement in low light? ______
  4. A dog has its chest down and its bottom up. What does it want? ______________
  5. Model it: draw arrows to show a dog seeing a ball, its brain getting the information, and the dog running to fetch it.

Answer key (for teachers)

  1. Two.
  2. Yellow and blue.
  3. Rod cells.
  4. To play (a play bow).
  5. Eyes (sense) → brain (processes the information) → legs and body (respond by running to the ball). This mirrors NGSS 4-LS1-2: senses, brain, response.

NGSS connection

  • 4-LS1-2: Use a model to describe that animals receive different types of information through their senses, process the information in their brain, and respond to the information in different ways.
