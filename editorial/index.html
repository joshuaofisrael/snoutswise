Editorial and sourcing policy

How SnoutsWise decides what to publish, which documents count as a source, and how to ask for a correction.

Who the byline is

Articles are signed SnoutsWise Editorial Team. That is the site, not a named person with a veterinary degree. We do not invent writers, clinic jobs or a review board. Reading a page is not a vet visit.

What we will cite

A fact is published only after the document it comes from has been opened. The documents we use are veterinary manuals and guidelines, welfare and poison control pages, kennel club explainers, and peer reviewed papers.

  • The Merck Veterinary Manual, AAHA guidelines, WSAVA charts and the National Research Council booklet on dog nutrition, for physiology, vaccines and feeding.
  • AVSAB position statements, the AVMA and the RSPCA, for behavior, training and welfare.
  • ASPCA Animal Poison Control, for foods and plants. On a toxic item the page leads with a call to your vet or a poison line. It does not give a home dose.
  • The American Kennel Club and The Royal Kennel Club, for breed groups and owner explainers.
  • A peer reviewed paper only after the DOI resolves and the abstract or the open full text has been read. A university newsroom can be the second source on a news piece. It is not enough on its own.

What we leave out

We do not invent numbers, quotations or case stories. We do not give a drug dose or a home treatment plan. A pet blog, a social post or a roundup that names no document is not a source for a health or behavior claim. If two solid sources disagree, the page says so.

How a page is built

The title answers the question. The first paragraph gives that answer. The rest says what the study or the guideline did, what it found, and where the evidence stops. Health pages include a block on when to call a vet. Each article lists its sources and shows a published date and a last reviewed date. The reviewed date moves when the words or the sources change, not when the site is rebuilt.

Images

A picture is either an AI generated illustration, labelled as such and linked to the image policy, or a licensed photograph credited on photo credits. We do not present a picture of a real person as a news photo of an event.

Corrections and ads

If a source has moved or a sentence is wrong, use the contact form and name the page and the source. We check it and correct the page. The site may show Google AdSense advertising. The ads are separate from the article and do not change the words. There are no affiliate links. See the disclaimer and the privacy policy.
